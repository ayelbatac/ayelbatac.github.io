Tuberculosis Research | Ayel L. R. Batac

Research on public health approaches to tuberculosis by Ayel L. R. Batac, focusing on program evaluation, implementation, and evidence to strengthen prevention and control.

TUBERCULOSIS

\(~\)

My research focuses on public health approaches to tuberculosis (TB) prevention and control. I am interested in how evidence can inform TB programs and how evaluation can guide improvements in practice. The broader objective is to support TB elimination by examining the gap between an intervention’s intended purpose and the experience of delivering that intervention.

A central concern is whether TB services meet the needs of the people they are intended to reach. The availability of screening does not, by itself, establish whether people can access care. Similarly, identifying someone who needs treatment does not resolve the challenges that may affect follow up. These distinctions inform my interest in how public health programs function beyond their immediate service outputs.

Research Focus

My research interests centre on the relationship between TB prevention strategies and public health practice. An intervention needs to be understood within the health system responsible for delivery. A screening initiative, for example, creates responsibilities for follow up that continue after screening has ended. Evaluating the initiative therefore requires consideration of how the additional activity fits within existing care.

Program evaluation provides a means of examining that relationship. Quantitative findings can describe participation and recorded outcomes. Evidence about implementation adds insight into the practical challenges encountered during delivery. Bringing these perspectives together can help identify where a response needs improvement without assuming that every outcome can be attributed to the intervention.

Community-informed practice is another important focus. I am interested in how community priorities can influence the design of TB services, rather than treating engagement solely as a means of increasing participation. Understanding how people experience care requires their perspectives to be sought directly. Descriptions of engagement activities cannot substitute for evidence about those experiences.

Methodological Approach

My approach combines epidemiological analysis with qualitative inquiry in the evaluation of public health interventions. Quantitative findings can describe program reach and recorded outcomes. Qualitative methods can examine how implementation unfolded and how people involved in an intervention understood that experience. Bringing these forms of evidence together helps place outcomes within the conditions of service delivery.

A particular interest is how evaluation can inform subsequent decisions. Quantitative patterns can identify where a program may require attention, while qualitative findings can help explain the circumstances behind those patterns. Together, the evidence can guide changes to service delivery. Recommendations need to remain proportionate to the study design, especially when an evaluation describes experience in a single setting. The aim is to support adaptation without assuming that one model of TB prevention will suit every community.

Contribution to Public Health

My research contributes knowledge about how TB interventions operate in practice. The emphasis is on making evaluation useful to those responsible for improving services, while recognizing the limits of what individual studies can establish.

  • Developing implementation evidence. Documenting the practical demands of TB interventions adds knowledge that outcome measures alone cannot provide. Evidence about delivery can help public health teams assess the preparation and support required for a proposed response.

  • Strengthening the interpretation of program reach. Participation totals describe the scale of an intervention, but can obscure gaps in access. Examining who remains unreached helps identify priorities for further investigation and more focused outreach.

  • Informing decisions about intervention design. Evaluation findings provide a basis for considering how additional interventions should complement existing TB services. The contribution is to support decisions grounded in the circumstances of care, rather than promote a uniform approach across settings.

Featured Publication

Planning and implementation of a community-wide tuberculosis screening intervention: a case study from Naujaat, Nunavut, Canada

This study provides a detailed account of how a community-wide screening (CWS) clinic was planned and implemented during a TB outbreak in Nunavut. The article explains how engagement with community leadership informed preparations for the clinic. It then connects screening and treatment outcomes with the operational experience of delivering the intervention. Lessons from an internal After Action Review offer practical insights for public health teams considering future CWS responses in remote settings.

Read the article

Xie XT, Batac ALR, Wills O, Kulleperuma K, Dhillon A, Palaniappan S, Agyemang EA. Planning and implementation of a community-wide tuberculosis screening intervention: a case study from Naujaat, Nunavut, Canada. Int J Circumpolar Health. 2026 Dec 31;85(1):2700154. doi: 10.1080/22423982.2026.2700154.
